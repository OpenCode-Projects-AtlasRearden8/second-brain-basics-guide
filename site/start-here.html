Start Here

A second brain is not a program you buy. It is a small habit of putting
thoughts in one trusted place, so your head is free to think instead of
remember.

Welcome. This manual teaches you how to keep a personal knowledge base
using plain text files on your own computer. No subscription, no cloud
account, no app you have to learn like a video game. If you can save a file,
you can do this.

Why plain files?

  • They outlive everything. A .txt or .md file from 1995 still opens

fine today. Apps come and go; text never dies.

  • They are yours. Your notes live in a folder you control. Back them up

like photos, and they are safe.

  • They are light. No loading screens, no logins, no " SYNCING… " bars.

A note opens as fast as you can read it.

Who this is for

Anyone who has too many open browser tabs in their brain. Shift workers,
students, writers, parents, anyone with a busy life and a full mental white-
board. Every chapter is written to be read in one short sitting — on a break,
on a commute, in a waiting room. Nothing here assumes you are a programmer.

The whole system in one paragraph

Keep one folder (the vault) with four areas: a daily note you open
every day (your landing pad), a fleeting pile for raw thoughts, a
projects shelf for the few things you are actively working on, and an
archive for everything finished or cold. Give files calm, boring names.
Link ideas together with simple wiki-style [[links]]. Spend fifteen
minutes a month tidying, not more. That is the entire method. The rest of
this manual just explains each piece and how to set it up.

Make the folder. Anywhere you like — Documents, Desktop, a synced drive.

my-second-brain/

That is it. In the next chapter we fill it with four folders and your first
daily note. Nothing fancier than that is required — it counts, and it is
the whole system already, just empty. Small steps count double when you
are tired.

*If you get stuck anywhere: your system does not need to be perfect to be
useful. A slightly messy notebook you actually open beats a beautiful one
you dread.*
