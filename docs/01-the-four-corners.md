# The Four Corners

Every note you will ever keep fits into one of four homes. Four is small
enough to remember on a bad day, which is the whole point.

## 1. The daily landing pad (`90-daily/`)

One file per day, named like `2026-10-27.md`. It is the first thing you open
and the last thing you close. Anything that arrives in your head during the
day lands here:

- things someone told you
- a half-formed idea from the shower
- "call the pharmacy" / "look up that word"
- the link your friend sent at 22:41

Think of it as the reception desk. Reception does not sort the mail. It just
catches everything so nothing is lost. At any point (same day is fine, week
is fine) you copy the bits that matter onward to their real homes — or let
the [[monthly-tidy]] sweep handle it.

Starter templates live in your vault under `templates/`, including a
[[daily-note]] template.

## 2. Fleeting notes (`00-inbox/`)

Also called a "flea circus" in some books — you may prefer "raw thoughts."
Fleeting notes are for when a thought is too big for today's page or too
small for a project: one idea, one file, written in one breath.

Rules (and they are gentle):

- Name can be rough: `idea-shift-handoffs.md`, `wants-ui-like-a-game.md`.
- No formatting rules at all. Bullet soup is a valid literary form.
- The inbox is allowed to be messy. It is a puddle, not a library.
- Anything still sitting there after a month either becomes a real note,
  gets archived, or gets deleted. All three outcomes are wins.

The inbox and the daily note do overlap, and that is fine. Use the daily
landing pad when a thought arrives mid-day; use the inbox when you sit down
to dump several at once (before bed, after a meeting, end of shift).

## 3. Project notes (`10-projects/`)

A project is *anything with an outcome and an end*: plan a move, write a
chapter, renew a license, rebuild the website, prep for a certification.
Each project gets one folder (or one file if it is small) with a date
prefix, like `2026-11-kdp-paperback-checklist.md`.

- Keep **few** projects in here — five or six active, max. More than that
  and the shelf stops helping.
- Everything else waits or lives in the archive. Out of sight is not out of
  mind when you can trust the archive.
- Inside a project note, put the next action at the top, in bold. When you
  reopen the file in three weeks, you should not have to reverse-engineer
  where you were. Future-you is a friendly stranger who needs a handover
  note.

## 4. The archive (`20-archive/`)

Where finished projects and old-but-worth-keeping notes go, organized by
year: `20-archive/2026/`. Nothing in the archive needs attention, which
makes it the calmest folder you own.

- When a project ends: move it in, one drag. Do not archive "someday" —
  just date it and put it in.
- Archive with confidence: references, past-jobs, course notes, receipts of
  thought. If you never need them, they cost nothing to keep. If you do
  need them, `Ctrl+F` still works.

## Why not one big pile?

Because "everything in one folder" turns file search into an archaeology
dig. Four corners give your brain four *doors*, and doors beat drawers. You
will know within two seconds which door a new thought belongs behind, and
if you do not, the daily note catches it — sorting is the machine's job or
future-you's job, not present-you's job.

## Do this now (5 minutes)

Inside your vault folder, make:

```text
my-second-brain/
  00-inbox/
  10-projects/
  20-archive/
  90-daily/
  90-daily/2026-10-27.md   ← today's landing pad
```

Write one line in today's daily note: `Today I started a second brain.`

 fancier than that is not required. It counts. It is the whole system
already — just empty.
