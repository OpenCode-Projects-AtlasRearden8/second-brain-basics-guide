# The Monthly Fifteen-Minute Tidy

> Once a month, fifteen minutes. No day-long deep-clean weekends. The timer
> is the boundary, and the boundary is the kindness.

This chapter assumes you have skipped a few days, or a few weeks. Good —
that was realistic. The monthly tidy is designed for *that* person, not for
an imaginary person who files everything daily.

Set a timer for 15 minutes. When it rings, stop. Whatever was not done
limps patiently until next month; that is the design.

## Minute 0–3: Empty pockets

- Open today's daily landing pad and the last three weeks of dailies.
- Anything that matters gets its one line copied out, with a `[[link]]`,
  to either a fleeting note, a project note, or nowhere-yet.
- Dailies themselves do **not** need to be organized. They *are* the
  archive for small days, and that is correct.

## Minute 3–7: The inbox sweep

Open `00-inbox/` and, for each note, pick the fastest honest action:

- **Promote** (it is a real idea): give it a proper name with the
  [[naming-that-survives-years]] rules, add a link or two. 10 seconds.
- **Attach** (it belongs to a project): move it into that project folder.
- **Archive** (it was worth keeping, finished or not): `20-archive/2026/`.
- **Delete** (it no longer means anything): gone. Deleting stale notes is
  tidying, not failure.

Three to nine notes is a *successful* sweep. You are not emptying the
puddle; you are keeping it a puddle instead of a bog.

## Minute 7–11: The project shelf

In `10-projects/`:

- Finished? Move it to `20-archive/2026/`. Give yourself the little
  "completed" click in your head — that is the reward the shelf is for.
- Dead (boring, no Longer)? Move it too, unchanged, same folder. "Someday"
  can live in the archive just as well.
- Still alive and more than six active? Pick the *two* you will actually
  touch before next month; mentally park the rest. Six is the ceiling, and
  it is a soft one.

## Minute 11–15: Connections + receipts

- Find notes missing links: in most Markdown editors that is the sum of
  notes with no outgoing `[[wiki-links]]`. Pick **two orphans** and give
  each them one link — the fastest way to grow the web from
  [[linking-ideas]].
- Community-catch-up moment: peek at "hub" notes (your Work/Life/etc.
  index pages). If one is stale, update one line. No heroics.
- Stand back, quarter-second: the vault exists, it has a pulse, next
  month's you inherits a cleaner desk than this month's you handed over.

## If fifteen minutes is too long on a bad month

Do the **2-minute version**: empty today's daily note's pockets into
wherever they obviously belong, close the lid. That is a legitimate tidy.
The system never demands hero days; it is built to absorb quiet months
without collapsing.

## Why "timer" is load-bearing

Without a timer, tidying expands into everything else you could be doing,
and next month you dread it. With one, tidying stays a small honest chore,
like brushing. Ten safe, boring, monthly strides beat one heroic leap you
never take again. (Low-energy days are part of the plan, not a failure of
it.)

## Do this now (1 minute)

Write `Tidy: 1st Saturday` on a calendar you actually see — or set the
recurring alarm now, while it is fresh. Future-you will accept the
appointment current-you made.
